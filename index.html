How it works, in one paragraph

A spout is never perfectly round. The jet leaves with the spout's slightly oval (or triangular, square) cross-section, and surface tension pulls it back toward a circle. Water has inertia, so it overshoots: the oval flips 90°, then flips back, a free oscillation at Rayleigh's frequency. The water moves down while it oscillates, so time becomes height: the oscillation is frozen into a steady pattern along the stream, one full flip-and-back every wavelength λ = 2πU/ω. Seen from the side the stream looks alternately wide and thin, and the curved faces that catch the room lights swap sides at every node. The bright reflection lines therefore cross, which is the X. Gravity speeds the water up and thins it, so the X's get longer as they go down, until the Rayleigh–Plateau instability breaks the stream into drops.

Every pixel here is a ray traced through the computed surface with Snell's law and Fresnel reflection (water n = 1.333). Nothing is drawn by hand except the spout. Equations, constants and every estimate are listed in the README.
